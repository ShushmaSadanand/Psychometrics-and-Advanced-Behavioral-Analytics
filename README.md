## Project: Psychometric Scale Engineering & Behavioral Causality Modeling (Digital Context Fatigue)
**File:** `Fatigue of Digital context.pdf` (Advanced Statistical Research Deck)

A rigorous psychometric research project that designs, tests, and validates a custom scale tracking **FFSW (Frequent Fragmentation of Structural Workflows)**—measuring the cognitive and emotional exhaustion caused by frequent digital multitasking—and models its downstream effects on consumer impulsiveness.

### Advanced Data Science & Statistical Architecture
* **Structural Scale Development Pipeline:** Generated an initial pool of over 26 qualitative items from a comprehensive literature review. Standardized items across 3 distinct latent cognitive domains:
  $$\text{Cognitive Disruption} \times \text{Work-Life Intertwining} \times \text{Emotional Exhaustion}$$
* **Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) & Item Purification:** Applied rigorous EFA matrices to audit cross-loadings and clean the item pool down to a finalized set of highly reliable questions, discarding redundant elements to guarantee construct validity.
* **Inferential Comparative Modeling (T-Tests & ANOVA):** Conducted independent sample t-tests and multi-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) to isolate variance in digital fatigue across different professional roles, work setups, and tenure brackets, determining baseline control variables.
* **Causal Interaction Pathways (Mediation & Moderation Models):** Built and analyzed structural interaction frameworks to decipher the exact psychological mechanisms driving consumer actions:
  * **Partial Mediation Framework:** Modeled the indirect effect of Social Media Exposure ($X$) on Impulsiveness ($Y$) via the mediator FOMO ($M$). The indirect pathway proved statistically significant ($\text{Estimate} = 0.113, p = 0.017$), operating alongside a commanding direct effect ($\text{Estimate} = 0.399, p < 0.001$).
  * **Trait-State-Behavior Cascade:** Tested a second robust partial mediation loop where FOMO ($X$) drives Shopping Information Overload ($M$), which in turn triggers Panic Buying / Impulse Purchasing ($Y$). The indirect pathway accounted for **37.6% of the total behavioral effect** ($\text{Indirect Effect} = 0.160, p < 0.001$).
  * **Moderation Matrix:** Evaluated the boundary conditions of user behavior, demonstrating that the relationship between digital platform usage and impulsive conversion is significantly moderated by an individual’s internal susceptibility to FOMO ($W$).
